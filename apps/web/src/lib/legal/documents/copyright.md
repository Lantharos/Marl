Marl respects the rights of creators. If you believe content on Marl infringes your copyright, you can ask us to take it down. If your content was taken down by mistake, you can file a counter notice. This process follows the notice-and-action rules of the EU Digital Services Act and the United States Digital Millennium Copyright Act.

## Before you file

Many copyright disputes can be resolved by talking to the repository owner, for example by opening an issue or asking them to add the attribution your licence requires. A takedown notice is a legal request; filing a false one can make you liable for damages.

Consider whether the use might be permitted by the licence you published under, by fair use, or by another exception. Code that merely implements the same idea, interface, or algorithm is usually not a copy of your work.

## Filing a takedown notice

Send your notice to legal@marl.sh, or use **Report** on the repository or file and choose **Copyright**. Include:

1. Your name, postal address, email address, and telephone number.
2. A description of the copyrighted work you believe was infringed, with a link to the original if it is online.
3. The exact Marl links to the content you want removed. For repositories with many files, say whether the whole repository infringes or only specific files.
4. A statement that you have a good faith belief that the use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
5. A statement that the information in the notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are the copyright owner or authorised to act on the owner's behalf.
6. Your physical or electronic signature.

## What happens next

We review every notice. If it is complete and valid, we disable access to the reported content and notify the repository owner with a copy of the notice, with your personal contact details removed except for your name. Where only specific files are infringing, we give the owner one business day to remove them before the whole repository is disabled.

We publish a record that content was disabled in response to a notice, without your contact details.

## Filing a counter notice

If your content was disabled and you believe it was a mistake or that you have the right to use it, send a counter notice to legal@marl.sh that includes:

1. Your name, postal address, email address, and telephone number.
2. The Marl links to the content that was disabled.
3. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you have a good faith belief the content was removed as a result of a mistake or misidentification.
4. A statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the courts where you live, or where Lantharos is established if you live elsewhere, and that you will accept service of process from the person who filed the original notice.
5. Your physical or electronic signature.

We forward valid counter notices to the person who filed the original notice. If they do not tell us within 10 to 14 business days that they have started legal proceedings, we restore the content.

## Repeat infringement

Accounts that repeatedly infringe copyright are suspended.
